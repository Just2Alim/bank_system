# Public Banking Technology Research

Research cut-off and access date: **2026-09-04**.

This evidence log informs the fictional Kazakhstan Banking Ecosystem Simulator. It does **not** claim access to private bank infrastructure, confidential designs, production credentials, or a certified connection to Kazakhstan's payment systems. Orda, Nomad, and Tengri are fictional banks; every person, account, card, alias, merchant, device, and transaction generated by the product is visibly synthetic.

## Method and confidence

Research preferred primary sources in this order: National Bank of Kazakhstan (NBK), National Payment Corporation of Kazakhstan (NPC/NPCK), ISO, official bank investor/career/product pages, and official software documentation. A vendor case study is retained only as secondary corroboration. Search-result summaries were not treated as evidence when a direct source was available.

- **CONFIRMED** — the linked source directly states the fact in the final column.
- **STRONG_INFERENCE** — public evidence supports a design direction but does not reveal a complete internal architecture.
- **SIMULATOR_DESIGN_CHOICE** — an explicit product decision, not a statement about a real institution.

Public vacancies are point-in-time evidence for a team or role. They do not establish an institution-wide stack. Product pages establish visible capabilities, not internal topology. The simulator therefore borrows patterns, never real brands, UI, data, or purported private designs.

## Architecture consequences

1. MSPD is represented as a liquidity-aware RTGS rail because NBK describes individual real-time gross settlement. The simulator does not claim protocol compatibility.
2. SMK is represented as small-value batch clearing with deterministic gross obligations and zero-sum multilateral net positions. The KZT 5 million public limit is configurable and tested.
3. MSMP is an addressing and orchestration layer using synthetic phone and QR aliases. It routes through the simulator's national platform and does not bypass central settlement accounting.
4. Interbank QR uses the explicit `SIMQR1` namespace with a local signature. It cannot be confused with a production national QR payload.
5. ISO support is a safe validated subset with external entities disabled, bounded payload size, explicit namespaces, and sanitized displays. The mapper is replaceable when a researched profile changes.
6. The three fictional banks have materially different implementation styles derived only from public signals: Orda legacy-hybrid, Nomad digital-native, and Tengri hybrid. Their databases, credentials, card processors, and ledgers remain isolated.
7. The national platform alone owns participants, aliases, RTGS queue, clearing cycles, ISO records, and central participant settlement positions.
8. The National Antifraud Center is mentioned only to explain a future consumer boundary. No antifraud behavior exists in code, schemas, generated personas, UI, or sample data.

## Explicit unknowns and limitations

- No source exposes a bank's complete private service topology, database layout, ledger, settlement adapter, security controls, or production version inventory.
- Vacancy pages can change or disappear and may describe only one team. Their access date is therefore part of the evidence.
- NPCK/NBK public pages establish concepts and service behavior, not permission to use production identifiers or connect to national systems.
- Message variants in the live ISO catalogue evolve. The simulator pins its own V1 profile and labels it non-certified instead of implying that the newest catalogue version is Kazakhstan's exact production profile.
- Every architecture statement not directly supported above is documented as a simulator choice in the ADRs.
